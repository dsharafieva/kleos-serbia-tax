Hiring in Serbia?
Here's where the money goes — and where the risk hides.

Serbian contractors are famously cheap to work with — a flat-rate freelancer can keep almost everything they invoice. But the same setup carries Serbia's signature trap: the independence test. This page shows both the money and the risk.

The system, in three charges

Low tax, heavy contributions, no VAT.

Serbia's income-tax rates are low and mostly flat. The weight sits in social contributions — and the flat-rate regime bundles both into one fixed monthly figure, while keeping you out of VAT entirely.

10%

Income tax — flat and low

Most routes tax income at a flat 10%. Companies pay 15% corporate tax, and a contractor reclassified as "dependent" is taxed as other income at 20% — but there's no steep progressive ladder like the EU.

Social contributions — the real weight

Pension (24%) and health (10.3%) contributions are where most of the money goes. Under the flat-rate regime these are folded into your fixed monthly amount; on other routes they scale with income.

VAT — but you're usually outside it

Standard VAT is 20%, yet flat-rate and small entrepreneurs stay out of the VAT system until turnover passes 8,000,000 RSD (~€68k). Note: that's a different, higher line than the €51k flat-rate ceiling — don't confuse them.

A year in the life of a Serbian contractor's taxes

Day 1
Register with the APR, get a PIB

Register as an entrepreneur (preduzetnik) or a company (DOO — from 100 RSD capital) with the Business Registers Agency; ~5 working days. Choose flat-rate or bookkeeping at sign-up.

Monthly (flat-rate)
Pay the fixed amount by the 15th

The tax office assesses your combined tax + contributions and sends it via ePorezi. No return to file — just log invoices in the KPO book and pay on time.

Quarterly (freelancer)
Self-report on the Tax Administration portal

Freelancers file and pay within 30 days of each quarter's end, choosing Model A or Model B whichever is cheaper for that quarter's income.

Watch continuously
Stay under the cap and pass the test

Keep annual income under 6,000,000 RSD to hold flat-rate status, and make sure each client relationship stays on the right side of the independence test.

The independence test decides everything.

Serbia's flat-rate regime is so cheap that companies used to push staff to register as "freelancers" to dodge payroll. The state's answer is the nine-criterion independence test (test samostalnosti). For each client relationship, if five or more criteria apply, the contractor is deemed dependent — and their income from that client is taxed as other income at 20% plus full contributions, far heavier than the flat rate. For a foreign client, the contractor self-reports; but the arrangement is the exposure.

Check every criterion that's true for one client relationship. Five or more, and the tax office can treat the contractor as a dependent worker of that client.

Which model they register

Flat-rate, freelancer and DOO don't converge — at the same income the burden can swing from under 15% to over 40%. Picking the model on "administrative ease" instead of the math is the most common and costly mistake.

How much they invoice

Because the flat-rate cost is fixed, the effective rate falls as invoicing rises toward the €51k cap — a paušalac near the ceiling can keep ~90% of what they bill. Great, until they cross it.

Crossing the €51k ceiling

Passing 6,000,000 RSD mid-year forces a switch to bookkeeping and triggers a penalty period to 31 December — tax and contributions on the whole profit, no personal-salary relief. A better model only opens on 1 January.

🧮

The personal-salary trick (lična zarada)

A bookkeeping entrepreneur who sets a low personal salary pays contributions on that small base and just 10% on the rest — the one route whose effective rate actually falls at higher income. Needs a good accountant.

🌍

Not being in the EU

Serbia borders the EU but isn't in it — so there's no intra-EU reverse charge, but a wide network of double-tax treaties covers most client countries. Payments and residence need a little more paperwork than an EU-to-EU deal.

The flat-rate amount is set by profession, municipality, age and gender — so two contractors billing the same can pay very different fixed sums. It's worth confirming the exact figure before you price a deal.
